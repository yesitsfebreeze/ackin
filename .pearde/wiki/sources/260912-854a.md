# A pegged syspolicyd clears when launchd respawns it under a new pid, and only syspolicyd needs it

On 2026-09-11 `/usr/libexec/syspolicyd` pid 498 pegged at 100% CPU and every
freshly linked Mach-O on the host hung in `_dyld_start` before `main` — the
fault recorded as
[[a-pegged-syspolicyd-is-a-machine-fault-that-looks-exactly-li]].

On 2026-09-12 at 16:11:51 the daemon was gone and `launchd` had respawned it as
pid 93109, sitting at 0.2% CPU. `amfid` was never restarted at all: pid 336 has
run unchanged since Fri 2026-09-11 13:00:08 and predates the peg. So the fault
is `syspolicyd` alone, and the second `killall amfid` in the documented remedy
is not load-bearing.

The discriminator is cheap and does not need root: link a one-line Rust binary
with `rustc` into a fresh `mktemp -d` and run it under `perl -e 'alarm 20;
exec(...)'`. rc=0 means the verdict path answers; rc=142 means it does not.
`rustc` running is not the signal — its own verdict was cached before the peg —
and neither is `cargo check` on a single file. What discriminates is anything
the build must *execute*: `build.rs` scripts and proc-macro dylibs.

Confirmed clear, all four stages bounded and green on the `zirkle` crate:
`rustc`+run rc=0, `cargo build` Finished, the freshly linked
`target/debug/zirkle --help` rc=0, `cargo test` 53 passed 0 failed, total wall
time under a minute. The peg had held eight attempts across three passes;
nothing an agent did cleared it, and no reboot happened. Waiting for the
daemon's own respawn is therefore a real fourth outcome alongside the three the
ticket offered, and the probe is what tells you it has arrived.
